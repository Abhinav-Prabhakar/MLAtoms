    05 · Probabilistic models/tier 3/3 variants/source

    Expectation propagation

    Intuition #

    gp_classification.py treated EP as a fact about Gaussian processes. It is not a fact about Gaussian processes. It is a recipe for any model that factorises into a Gaussian prior and a product of scalar likelihoods:

    \[p(\theta) \propto N(\theta;0,\Sigma_0)\prod_{i=1}^{n} f_i(a_i^\top\theta) \qquad (1)\]

    Bayesian logistic regression, a Gaussian process with a robust likelihood, a probit model with correlated coefficients, a factor graph whose edges are observations — all of them are (1) with a different a_i and a different f_i. EP is one algorithm that runs on all of them.

    The recipe is: replace each factor with a Gaussian, adjust the Gaussians until the resulting posterior has the right marginals. A single Gaussian site in front of factor \(i\),

    \[t_i(a_i^\top\theta) = \tilde Z_i\,N(a_i^\top\theta;\,\nu_i,\,1/\tau_i) \qquad (2)\]

    is a claim that factor \(i\) carries a certain amount of information (\(\tau_i\)), centred at a certain value (\(\nu_i\)), with a certain total mass (\(\tilde Z_i\)). Multiply them all onto the prior and the posterior is a Gaussian, exactly, by completing the square. The only question is what those numbers should be.

            Σ₀, {aᵢ, yᵢ}
             │
             ▼
      q(θ) = N(μ, Σ) ──────────────────────────────┐
             │                                     │
             │ divide out site i (a subtraction)   │
             ▼                                     │
        cavity q₋ᵢ ──── × fᵢ(yᵢ | aᵢᵀθ) ───► tilted q̂ᵢ
             │                                     │
             │ more uncertain than q,              │
             │ and pulled by the likelihood        │
             ▼                                     │
        match mean and variance of q̂ᵢ              │
             │                                     │
             ▼                                     │
        new site (τᵢ, νᵢ) ── damped ────────────────┘

    The reason to match the marginal rather than the joint is that the marginal is what you report. A posterior over forty weights is forty one-dimensional summaries plus a covariance; getting the joint KL right while getting every marginal wrong is not a win in practice, and that is exactly the failure mode of the mean-field bound in variational_inference.py.

    The three Gaussian approximations in this chapter are distinguished by what they match and where:

    matches cost guarantee
    Laplace the joint log-density, at its mode one Hessian exact in the \(n\to\infty\) limit
    mean-field VI a factorised \(q\), by \(\mathrm{KL}(q\lVert p)\) an optimiser a lower bound on \(\log Z\)
    EP every marginal, by moments a fixed-point loop none

    There is no bound here, no Lyapunov function, and no promise that the loop converges. What EP has instead is accuracy on the quantity people ask for, which is why it is the default in production systems — TrueSkill's matchmaking model is EP, and so is the layerwise Bayesian neural network in Hernández-Lobato & Adams (2015).

    Two numbers from the demo set the scale. On Bayesian logistic regression with 40 points and two weights, EP's posterior mean is within 0.001 of an exact 2-D grid integral and its covariance within 0.003, its evidence within 0.0047 nats, and it gets there in 9 sweeps. And with a Gaussian likelihood it reproduces GP regression to \(2\times10^{-13}\) — the invariant that catches an error in the site algebra, because in that case EP is not approximating anything.

    Variants #

    Variant Factor a_i Scheduling Reading
    ep logistic a data row \(x_i\) full steps Bayesian logistic regression
    damped_ep logistic a data row \(x_i\) \(\delta = 0.7\) the same fit, stabilised
    ep_for_gp Student-t a basis vector \(e_i\) full steps robust GP regression

    ep and damped_ep are the same model. Damping interpolates between the old site and the new one, so it changes the path and not the destination — the demo runs both on four nearly collinear points and they reach the same mean \([1.246, 0.065]\) and the same \(\log Z = -1.8650\). What damping costs is iterations, and the cost is measurable:

    damping 1.0 0.7 0.5 0.3
    sweeps 8 25 41 75

    Roughly \(1/\delta\), because a shorter step has to cover the same distance. On this problem full steps happen to converge, so damping buys nothing but robustness — the point of the variant is that on the problems where full steps oscillate, it is the difference between an answer and a loop that never ends. It is a safety net, not an accelerator, and a table that presented it as faster would be lying.

    The third variant is where EP stops being about regression coefficients:

    parameter space    a_i = x_i    the factor sees one row of data
function space     a_i = e_i    the factor sees one coordinate of θ

    With \(a_i = e_i\) the prior covariance is the kernel matrix \(K\) and \(\theta\) is the vector of latent function values at the training points. That is GP regression, and ep_for_gp is the GP-classification machinery of gp_classification.py with the likelihood swapped for a Student-t. The code does not branch on which one it is; A=None means "one factor per coordinate", and A being the data matrix means the other.

    Two more likelihoods are reachable through the likelihood= kwarg and are not variants, because they are controls rather than models:

    • gaussian — the exactness test. With \(f_i(y_i\mid\eta) = N(y_i;\eta,\sigma^2)\) the site is the likelihood, EP is not an approximation, and the demo asserts the sites come out as \(\tau_i = 1/\sigma^2\), \(\nu_i = y_i\) to \(10^{-10}\).
    • probit — the closed forms. Probit sites have analytic tilted moments, so it is the one non-Gaussian likelihood where nothing in the loop is quadrature.

    Derivation #

    1. A Gaussian prior times Gaussian sites #

    Start from (1) with a Gaussian prior \(N(\theta;0,\Sigma_0)\) and the \(n\) sites (2). Then

    \[q(\theta) \propto N(\theta;0,\Sigma_0)\prod_{i=1}^{n}N(a_i^\top\theta;\nu_i,1/\tau_i) \qquad (3)\]

    Each site is an exponential of a quadratic in the projection, so the product is an exponential of a quadratic in \(\theta\):

    \[\Lambda = \Sigma_0^{-1}+\sum_i\tau_i a_i a_i^\top, \qquad \Sigma=\Lambda^{-1}, \qquad \mu=\Sigma b, \qquad b=\sum_i\tau_i\nu_i a_i \qquad (4)\]
    \[q(\theta)=N(\theta;\mu,\Sigma) \qquad (5)\]

    That is the whole of the easy half: given the sites, (4) is two matrix products and one solve. Every hard part of EP is in choosing \(\nu\) and \(\tau\).

    2. The cavity, by subtraction #

    To decide site \(i\)'s value, EP asks what the rest of the model believes about \(a_i^\top\theta\) — the posterior with site \(i\) removed. In natural parameters that is a subtraction of one rank-one precision and one linear term:

    \[\Lambda_{-i}=\Lambda-\tau_i a_i a_i^\top, \qquad \Sigma_{-i}=\left(\Lambda_{-i}\right)^{-1} \qquad (6)\]

    Sherman–Morrison on the rank-one removal gives everything in terms of the current \(q\), with no matrix built. With \(s_i = a_i^\top\Sigma a_i\):

    \[\Sigma_{-i}=\Sigma+\frac{\tau_i\,\Sigma a_i a_i^\top\Sigma}{1-\tau_i s_i}, \qquad s_{-i}^2=\frac{s_i}{1-\tau_i s_i} \qquad (7)\]
    \[m_{-i}=s_{-i}^2\left(\frac{a_i^\top\Sigma b}{s_i}-\tau_i\nu_i\right) \qquad (8)\]

    Equation (7) is why the code never forms a cavity covariance: the mean and the variance of the projection are three dot products, and \(1/(1-\tau_i s_i)\) is the only scalar that can misbehave.

    3. The tilted distribution, and the site update #

    Multiply the cavity by the true factor and renormalise:

    \[\hat q_i(\eta)=\frac{1}{\hat Z_i}\,N(\eta;\,m_{-i},\,s_{-i}^2)\,f_i(y_i\mid\eta), \qquad \eta=a_i^\top\theta \qquad (9)\]

    \(q\) will be \(q_{-i}\) times the site \(t_i\), so requiring \(q\)'s projection to have \(\hat q_i\)'s first two moments fixes the site uniquely:

    \[\tau_i^{\text{new}}=\frac{1}{\hat\sigma_i^2}-\frac{1}{s_{-i}^2}, \qquad \nu_i^{\text{new}}=\frac{1}{\tau_i^{\text{new}}}\left(\frac{\hat\mu_i}{\hat\sigma_i^2}-\frac{m_{-i}}{s_{-i}^2}\right) \qquad (10)\]

    The bracket in (10) is the site's natural linear parameter \(\eta_i=\tau_i\nu_i\); \(\nu_i=\eta_i/\tau_i\) is its mean. Storing \(\eta\) where the code expects \(\nu\) leaves the posterior covariance exactly right and the mean wrong by a factor of \(\tau_i\) — a bug that survives every variance check and is caught only by a case with a closed form.

    Putting the site back with a rank-one update is also Sherman–Morrison:

    \[\Sigma\leftarrow\Sigma-\frac{\Delta\tau_i}{1+\Delta\tau_i s_i}\,\Sigma a_i a_i^\top, \qquad b\leftarrow b+\Delta(\tau_i\nu_i)\,a_i \qquad (11)\]

    so a site update costs \(O(d^2)\) and a sweep costs \(O(nd^2)\), with no \(O(d^3)\) anywhere after the initial Cholesky.

    4. Damping #

    EP as described is a fixed-point iteration with no descent property. In practice the update is shortened:

    \[\tau_i\leftarrow(1-\delta)\tau_i+\delta\,\tau_i^{\text{new}}, \qquad \nu_i\leftarrow(1-\delta)\nu_i+\delta\,\nu_i^{\text{new}} \qquad (12)\]

    With \(\delta=1\) this is the textbook algorithm and it can oscillate; \(\delta=0.7\) is the usual compromise. Because a damped step is a shorter step in the same direction, the fixed points of the map do not change — only the route. That is a claim worth asserting rather than believing, and the demo does.

    5. The evidence #

    The point of EP is the posterior, but its normaliser is a usable by-product. The unnormalised \(q\) in (3) integrates to

    \[Z_q=\left(\prod_i\tilde Z_i\right)\int N(\theta;0,\Sigma_0)\prod_iN(a_i^\top\theta;\nu_i,1/\tau_i)\,d\theta \qquad (13)\]

    The product of sites is a Gaussian in \(\theta\) with precision \(\sum_i\tau_i a_ia_i^\top=\Lambda-\Sigma_0^{-1}\) and linear term \(b\), so

    \[\int N(\theta;0,\Sigma_0)\,e^{-\frac12\theta^\top(\Lambda-\Sigma_0^{-1})\theta+b^\top\theta}\,d\theta=\frac{\lvert\Sigma\rvert^{1/2}}{\lvert\Sigma_0\rvert^{1/2}}\,e^{\frac12b^\top\Sigma b} \qquad (14)\]

    and with the \((2\pi)^{-n/2}\prod_i\tau_i^{1/2}\) from the site normalisations:

    \[\log Z_{\text{EP}}=\sum_i\log\tilde Z_i+\frac12\sum_i\log\tau_i-\frac{n}{2}\log2\pi-\frac12\log\lvert\Sigma_0\rvert+\frac12\log\lvert\Sigma\rvert+\frac12b^\top\Sigma b-\frac12\sum_i\tau_i\nu_i^2 \qquad (15)\]

    Three constants in (15) are easy to get wrong and the demo found all three: the \(d\)-dimensional \((2\pi)^{-d/2}\) of the prior and the \((2\pi)^{d/2}\) of the Gaussian integral cancel, leaving one \(-(n/2)\log2\pi\); \(\lvert\Lambda\rvert\) is \(\lvert\Sigma\rvert^{-1}\), so the determinant term is \(+\tfrac12\log\lvert\Sigma\rvert\); and the \(-\tfrac12\sum_i\tau_i\nu_i^2\) is not optional.

    6. The amplitude, which moment matching leaves free #

    Equation (10) fixes the shape of the site and says nothing about its mass. The condition that fills the gap is that the site must carry the same mass as the factor it stands in for, measured under the cavity:

    \[\int q_{-i}(\eta)\,t_i(\eta)\,d\eta=\tilde Z_i\,N(\nu_i;\,m_{-i},\,s_{-i}^2+1/\tau_i)\overset{!}{=}\hat Z_i \qquad (16)\]
    \[\tilde Z_i=\frac{\hat Z_i}{N(\nu_i;\,m_{-i},\,s_{-i}^2+1/\tau_i)} \qquad (17)\]

    Without (17) the evidence is not even exact in the Gaussian case, which is the single cheapest test for it. With (17) it is, and the one-site case becomes exact for a second reason: with \(n=1\) the cavity is the prior, so \(\hat Z_1=p(y_1)\) and the two Gaussian terms in (15) cancel by symmetry.

    7. The tilted moments, for four factors #

    \(f_i(y\mid\eta)\) \(\hat\mu_i\) \(\hat\sigma_i^2\) \(\hat Z_i\)
    \(N(y;\eta,\sigma^2)\) \(\hat\sigma^2\left(\frac{m}{s^2}+\frac{y}{\sigma^2}\right)\) \(\left(\frac{1}{s^2}+\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\right)^{-1}\) \(N(y;m,s^2+\sigma^2)\)
    \(\Phi(y\eta)\) \(m+\frac{y s^2 r}{\sqrt{1+s^2}}\) \(s^2-\frac{s^4 r(z+r)}{1+s^2}\) \(\Phi(z)\)
    \(\sigma(y\eta)\) quadrature quadrature quadrature
    \(t_\nu\!\left(\frac{y-\eta}{\sigma}\right)\) quadrature quadrature quadrature

    with, for the probit row,

    \[z=\frac{ym}{\sqrt{1+s^2}}, \qquad r=\frac{\varphi(z)}{\Phi(z)} \qquad (18)\]

    and \(m,s^2\) standing for the cavity mean and variance. The probit row is the truncated-normal formulas: a Gaussian times a Gaussian CDF is not Gaussian, but its first two moments are elementary, which is why probit has no quadrature in it anywhere. The logistic and Student-t rows have no closed form in the mean either, so the code integrates them with 40-point Gauss–Hermite.

    The Gaussian row deserves a second look. There the tilted variance is \((1/s^2+1/\sigma^2)^{-1}\), so the new site precision is

    \[\tau^{\text{new}}=\frac{1}{\hat\sigma^2}-\frac{1}{s^2}=\frac{1}{\sigma^2} \qquad (19)\]

    independent of the cavity, and the new site mean is \(y\). The site is the likelihood, exactly. EP recovers GP regression because in that case it is not approximating: the first sweep writes down the right sites, the second confirms they do not move, and the answer is exact. The demo checks the sites against \(1/\sigma^2\) to \(10^{-10}\) and the posterior against the closed form to \(2\times10^{-13}\).

    8. When the site is not a Gaussian #

    Everything above assumed \(\hat q_i\) has a moment-matched Gaussian site, which means assuming \(\hat\sigma_i^2\le s_{-i}^2\) so that (10) gives \(\tau_i\ge0\). A log-concave factor guarantees that. A Student-t does not:

          cavity N(η; m, s²)            tilted ∝ cavity × t((y−η)/σ)
      ╭────╮                        ╭─╮        ╭─╮
     ╱      ╲                      ╱   ╲      ╱   ╲
    ────────────                  ────────────────────
        m                        m          y

    When the residual is large the tilt has two bumps — one where the prior is, one where the observation is — and the Gaussian that matches its first two moments is wider than the cavity. Then \(\tau_i<0\): the site is not a probability distribution, only an algebraic object that widens \(q\) along \(a_i\). That is precisely the mechanism that makes the fit robust: a negative site precision is the algebra's way of saying "this point is not evidence, ignore it". But the factorisation in (13) no longer describes a product of densities, so the evidence is undefined. The code reports the posterior and sets logZ_ to nan.

    Code walkthrough #

    The Gaussian tilt is (19) in code, and its lack of dependence on the cavity is visible: s2 appears only in the sum 1/s2 + 1/noise**2.

            if self.likelihood == "gaussian":
            # the one case with a closed form in every direction, and the
            # reason EP must reproduce GP regression exactly
            prec = 1.0 / s2 + 1.0 / self.noise**2
            mean = (m / s2 + y / self.noise**2) / prec
            lz = (-0.5 * LOG2PI - 0.5 * math.log(s2 + self.noise**2)
                  - 0.5 * (y - m) ** 2 / (s2 + self.noise**2))
            return mean, 1.0 / prec, lz

    The probit tilt is (18) plus the truncated-normal moments. r is \(\varphi/\Phi\), computed as an exponential of a difference because both factors underflow in the left tail.

            if self.likelihood == "probit":
            z = y * m / math.sqrt(1.0 + s2)
            lz = math.log(0.5 * math.erfc(-z / math.sqrt(2.0)))
            r = math.exp(-0.5 * z * z - 0.5 * LOG2PI - lz)
            mean = m + y * s2 * r / math.sqrt(1.0 + s2)
            var = s2 - s2 * s2 * r * (z + r) / (1.0 + s2)
            return mean, var, lz

    Everything else — logistic, Student-t — goes through one quadrature branch. The two likelihoods share every line except the one that evaluates the factor, which is why likelihood="student_t" versus "logistic" is a one-word change.

            x, w = np.polynomial.hermite.hermgauss(40)
        eta = m + math.sqrt(2.0 * s2) * x
        if self.likelihood == "logistic":
            lik = sigmoid(y * eta)
        else:
            # Student-t on the residual: p(y|η) ∝ (1 + z²/ν)^(−(ν+1)/2)
            z = (y - eta) / self.noise
            lik = (1.0 + z * z / self.dof) ** (-0.5 * (self.dof + 1.0))
        den = float((w * lik).sum()) * INV_SQRT_PI

    The sweep is equations (7)–(12) in order: the cavity variance and mean as two scalars, the tilt, the site in natural form, damping, then a rank-one update of \(\Sigma\) instead of a new factorisation.

                    a = A[i]
                s = float(a @ Sigma @ a)                    # Var(aᵀθ) under q
                denom = 1.0 - tau[i] * s
                if denom <= 1e-12:                          # cavity would be improper
                    delta = max(delta, 1.0)
                    continue
                s2 = s / denom
                m = s2 * (float(a @ (Sigma @ b)) / s - tau[i] * nu[i])
                m_t, s2_t, _ = self._tilted(m, s2, y[i])
                tau_new = 1.0 / s2_t - 1.0 / s2
                eta_new = m_t / s2_t - m / s2
                nu_new = eta_new / tau_new

                d_ = self.damping
                tau_i = (1.0 - d_) * tau[i] + d_ * tau_new
                nu_i = (1.0 - d_) * nu[i] + d_ * nu_new

    nu_new divides by tau_new: the subtraction produces the natural parameter \(\eta\), and the stored parameter is the mean. Dropping that division is the invisible bug described under (10).

    The rank-one update is (11), and the guard scale > 1e-12 is the same impropriety check as above — a negative \(\Delta\tau\) is legal, a step that would make \(\Lambda\) singular is not.

                    # rank-one update of Σ in O(d²), Sherman–Morrison with a
                # negative semi-definite step; b = Σ τ_iν_i a_i tracks the mean
                dt = tau_i - tau[i]
                scale = 1.0 + dt * s
                if abs(dt) > 0.0 and scale > 1e-12:
                    Sig_a = Sigma @ a
                    Sigma = Sigma - (dt / scale) * np.outer(Sig_a, Sig_a)
                b = b + (tau_i * nu_i - tau[i] * nu[i]) * a

    The evidence is (15) and (17). The amplitude loop rebuilds each cavity once more — the sites have all moved since the sweep — and the final expression is one line per term of (15).

                    v = s2 + 1.0 / tau[i]
                log_nu = -0.5 * LOG2PI - 0.5 * math.log(v) - 0.5 * (nu[i] - m) ** 2 / v
                z_hat += lz - log_nu
            # the `(2π)^{-d/2}` of the prior and the `(2π)^{d/2}` of the Gaussian
            # integral cancel, leaving one `−(n/2)log2π` for the n sites
            self.logZ_ = (z_hat - 0.5 * self.n * LOG2PI
                          + 0.5 * float(np.log(tau).sum())
                          + 0.5 * logdet - 0.5 * logdet0
                          + 0.5 * float(b @ self.mu_)
                          - 0.5 * float((tau * nu**2).sum()))

    Negative precision is handled by refusing to compute the evidence at all, rather than by returning a number that came from a factorisation that does not apply.

    Complexity #

    \(n\) factors, \(d\) parameters, \(T\) sweeps, one factor revisited per step:

    Step Time Memory
    prior Cholesky \(O(d^3)\) \(O(d^2)\)
    cavity + tilt \(O(d^2)\) per site \(O(d)\)
    rank-one site update \(O(d^2)\) per site \(O(d^2)\)
    one sweep \(O(nd^2)\) \(O(d^2)\)
    full fit \(O(nd^2T)\) \(O(d^2+nd)\)
    prediction on \(m\) points \(O(md^2)\) \(O(md)\)

    In parameter space \(d\ll n\) and the fit is linear in the data: 40 points with two weights takes 9 sweeps in milliseconds. In function space \(d=n\) and one sweep is \(O(n^3)\) — the same cost as one Newton step in gp_classification.py, and that file's 25 damped sweeps are the same \(Tn^3\) budget. Nothing here improves on the cubic; the sparse variants (inducing points, A replaced by a low-rank factor) are what change it.

    The one expensive operation that is avoided is the reason the update is written as Sherman–Morrison rather than as a refactorisation: with \(d=n\), a naive implementation would do \(n\) Cholesky factorisations per sweep instead of one matrix–vector update per site.

    Failure modes #

    Non-convergence, and multiple fixed points. The loop is a fixed-point iteration with no objective being decreased, so it can oscillate, and where it converges it can converge to different answers from different orderings. The demo's schedules land on the same fixed point but that is a property of the problem, not a theorem. Diagnostic: history_ — a delta that plateaus above tol is oscillation, not slowness. Lower the damping before raising n_iter.

    A negative site precision. A non-log-concave factor (Student-t, a mixture, a bimodal observation model) can make \(\hat\sigma_i^2>s_{-i}^2\), so \(\tau_i<0\) and the site is not a density. The posterior can still be a legitimate robust fit; the evidence cannot be computed and the code returns nan. Treat any negative \(\tau_i\) as a statement about the factor, not about the data. Diagnostic: logZ_valid_, and which sites are negative — a single one out of many is a different situation from half of them.

    A vanishing cavity variance. \(s_{-i}^2=s_i/(1-\tau_i s_i)\) has \(1-\tau_i s_i\) in a denominator, and for a site that has become near-deterministic the two terms agree to within rounding. The code clamps and skips; too many skips means a silently truncated fit. Diagnostic: count how often denom <= 1e-12 fires by instrumenting the loop; if it is not zero, the result is not EP.

    Sites that should be zero. With a logistic or probit factor and a point that is well explained, \(\tau_i\) legitimately shrinks toward zero and the site becomes uninformative. With a Gaussian likelihood it never does. The failure is not the small precision but 1/tau[i] in the amplitude step, which is why that step is inside the logZ_valid_ guard.

    The evidence is not a bound. Laplace and EP both report a number for \(\log p(y)\), and neither is variational, so neither is guaranteed to sit below the truth. On the demo's two-point problem Laplace is 0.014 nats off and EP 0.00001; on a misspecified model they can disagree by many nats in either direction. Never read a 0.5-nat gap between two EP runs as evidence for anything.

    Overconfidence under misspecification. EP matches moments of \(\hat q_i\), not of \(p\), and when the factor is wrong (an outlier, a mislabelled point, the wrong kernel) the matched moments are the wrong target and the posterior variance comes out too small. The Student-t variant exists because that is the usual fix: change the factor until a Gaussian site can describe it.

    The invisible parameterisation bug. (10) has one division that moment-matching tests cannot see, because it does not affect the covariance. Any EP implementation should be tested on the Gaussian likelihood first, where the correct sites are known in closed form, and on a one-site problem, where EP is exact by theorem.

    \(n\) in the tens of thousands. Function-space EP is \(O(Tn^3)\) and 25 sweeps make that unusable past a few thousand points. Sparse EP, like sparse GP regression, replaces the kernel matrix with inducing points; the sites survive the substitution unchanged.

    Applications #

    Bayesian logistic and probit regression. (1) with \(a_i=x_i\) is the model, and EP's posterior mean agrees with the exact grid to 0.001 on the demo's data. The practical payoff over a Laplace fit is calibrated uncertainty on small data — the same situation where laplace_approximation.py is most overconfident.

    Gaussian process classification. EP is the standard inference for GP classification and the one that gp_classification.py implements natively; this file shows that it is a special case of a general loop rather than a GP-specific derivation.

    Robust regression. A Student-t likelihood on the residual gives a fit where one bad point becomes a negative-precision site instead of a large residual — the demo's contaminated series goes from RMSE 0.423 to 0.238 with one outlier moved. Heavy-tailed observation models are the natural home of this file.

    Skill rating and matchmaking. TrueSkill models a player as a Gaussian and one game as a difference of two Gaussians plus a probit; the update is EP's one-factor update, and a ladder of millions of players is a factor graph that gets swept. This is EP as production software, not as statistics.

    Layerwise Bayesian neural networks. Probabilistic backpropagation treats each layer's weights as independent Gaussian sites and propagates moments forward and backward; the backward pass is EP on the layer's output factor. It is the reason EP is in the toolbox of people who would otherwise say "we use variational inference".

    Decoding and factor graphs. Turbo codes, LDPC decoding, and Gaussian belief propagation are EP with different factors; the "approximate message passing" family in signal processing is EP's fixed-point analysis applied to the same updates. Minka's 2001 paper frames all of it as one divergence-minimisation procedure.

    Where it is a trap. Factors that are not unimodal or not close to Gaussian (mixtures, hard constraints, combinatorial likelihoods), models where the evidence is the deliverable and a bound is required, and anything where the site parameterisation is not checked against a closed-form case first.
